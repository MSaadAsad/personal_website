The Jhelum Marches

 Orders of the day

Before you march

Turns

The two sides take turns. In your command, march and fight with each of your formations, then End command (E). Each round is one day. Main events (a column arriving, a post taken, a general falling) come as dispatches with their history.

March

Select a formation and click blue ground. Each hex is 1 km; foot have 12 march points a day, horse 18–21. Roads cost 1 a hex, open plain 2, broken ground 3, hills 4. Stepping next to an enemy ends the march. Two formations may share a hex.

Fight

Click a red ring to attack, or a dashed one to march and attack. Hover first to see the odds. Each side rolls a d6 plus modifiers for ground, works, the general, numbers round the defender, fatigue and supply. Down to two figures (400 men) a formation falls back and cannot attack until it rallies; cut off, it surrenders. Formations rally only resting in a village, town or fort.

Guns & walls

Field guns fire 2 km at what they can see. Settlements and forts add defence and great fortresses have walls on top: guns batter them down, and foot next to them can dig a mine (M). Foot can also dig a kacha fort (K) where they stand, in open country or round a village or town.

Supply

Every command your formations eat from their stock, and wagons from the places you hold bring more. Hungry troops fight worse and in the end desert. Guard your roads, and cut the enemy’s.

Sight & the general

With fog of war on you see only what your troops can see; faded markers show where the enemy was last seen. Your general gives the formation he leads +1 attack and defence: G to ride to another, F for a forced march.

 Field manual

How to command

Win

At the end of every round each strongpoint (◆) scores a war point for its holder. First to the target with the lead wins, and so does holding them all; at the round limit the higher score wins. Most campaigns have their own goal instead, shown when you start. Wiping out the enemy never wins by itself: the places the campaign is fought over decide it.

March

Select a formation and click blue ground. You can split a march across several clicks. Each hex is 1 km across. Roads cost 1 per hex, open plain 2, broken ground 3, hills 4 and mountains 6; a foot formation has 12 march points a day, so about 12 km by road or 6 km across open country; horse 18–21. Scrub and forest add 1, horse pays extra in rough ground, and guns pay extra in hills.

Rivers

Great rivers (the Jhelum and Chenab) can only be crossed at a ghat (ferry bar on the map). Once per campaign a foot formation on the bank can build a ferry: it costs that formation its command and 200 men, and afterwards either army may cross there. Fordable rivers such as the Poonch cost +3, and nalas +1. Defenders behind water gain defence.

Contact

Stepping next to an enemy ends your march. Formations may share a hex two at a time: with a formation selected, click a friendly hex within its march to join that stack (Shift-click to select the formation there instead).

Fight

Every formation is about 1,000 men, shown as five figures of 200 on its stand; losses take figures off. Click a red ring to attack, or a dashed ring to march and attack in one order. Hover first to see the odds and expected losses. Each side rolls a d6 and adds modifiers. Win by 3+ to smash (600 men lost); tie or win by less to strike (400), with 200 lost back in melee. Every 800 enemy men standing next to the defender cost it 1 defence (up to 3), and a formation encircled (enemies on three sides, or on opposite sides) loses 1 more and surrenders rather than falling back. Lose by 1–2 in melee and both lose 200; lose by 3+ and you are repulsed. A formation deals damage in proportion to the soldiers it has left. Settlements give defence (villages, towns and sarais +1, cities +2, forts +3) and great fortresses have walls on top. Guns wear all of these down: every bombardment chips away part of a level and a hit brings one down, the great walls first, and all guns get a bonus against works still standing. A defender 60 m above its attacker gets +1 (+2 at 180 m), and attacking downhill gives the attacker +1. Cavalry is poor wherever the ground rises more than 100 m within a hex: +2 to march there, −1 attacking into it and −1 defending in it. Field guns (light field guns, horse artillery, heavy batteries) fire two hexes (2 km); camel swivels and jingals must close to the next hex like everyone else. Guns firing at range and skirmishers risk nothing.

Fatigue

An attack takes a quarter of a foot formation's day (3 march points), and a formation needs that much left to make one. Afterwards foot and guns are done for the day; only horse can strike and ride on with what remains (a raid). A formation that has not marched in its latest command fights at +1 (holding its ground). One that used more than a third of its march fights at −1, more than two thirds at −2, both attacking and when attacked before its next command.

Sight

With fog of war on, you see only what your formations can see: every hex with a clear line from the watcher’s eye to a man standing there, over the surveyed ground and the curve of the earth. On flat ground a man on foot sees another man’s head to about 10 km, but a hill, a wall or a tower sees much further, and woods and settlements block the view. The eye makes out a formation to about 10 km (12 km for horse, 16 km from high walls or a hill), and a column that marched in its latest command raises dust that shows 4 km beyond that. Troops in woods, a settlement or a kacha fort are seen only from 1.5 km, so you may see the enemy before he sees you. Held strongpoints and forts keep a 3 km watch. Ground out of sight is shaded; faded markers show where enemy formations were last seen, or reported at the outset. Guns fire only at what they can see, and a march that runs into an enemy it could not see halts on contact.

Dig in

A foot formation that has not moved or fought, out of the enemy’s reach, can spend its whole day digging a kacha fort (K): a mud wall and ditch round its hex, in open country or round a village or town without standing walls, +2 defence to whoever holds it (on top of the village or town's own). Guns wear it down like any other works.

Foot next to enemy-held works can dig a mine (M) under them: three whole days' digging, and on the third it is sprung and brings down two levels of the works at once. Keep a formation at the gallery's mouth, or it is lost. Foot inside the works can countermine (M) for a day, and break into the gallery on a 4 or better. Only foot dig forts, mines and ferries.

Retreat & rally

A formation pushed down to two figures (400 men) falls back up to two hexes and cannot attack until it rallies. If it is cut off it surrenders. A formation that neither marches nor fights, away from the enemy and resting in a village, sarai, town, city or fort, rallies 200 men, or 400 near the general. Out on open ground it does not.

The general

Your general rides with one formation: +1 attack, +1 defence, and it never falls back. Once per command he can take command of another formation within 24 km (G) and order a forced march for one within 8 km (F). If his formation is destroyed he rides off to the nearest friends within 24 km; only if it was surrounded, or no one is near, is he captured, and the enemy gains 5 war points.

Supply & forts

Every formation carries a supply stock. Each command it eats 20%, and wagons from your supply zone (the strongpoints, forts and allied seats you hold, and any home base) bring in more: 40% at a depot, about 20% two wagon-days away, nothing beyond three and a half days or if enemies cut the road. Below 60% a formation attacks at −1; below 40% it is hungry (−1 defence too, no rallying); below 20% its men desert each command until it marches back. Forts belong to whoever last garrisoned them. A fort or town the enemy has closed on from two or more sides is besieged: it supplies no one, its garrison lives on half rations from its magazines, and it starves over a week or two. Tick Supply under the minimap to see where your wagons reach.

Chronicle

Fights close together in place and time make one engagement, named after the nearest place: a battle, an assault, or a siege if a fort is invested for days. When one ends, Claude writes its account (the turning points, what it cost) from what happened on the map. Add your own Anthropic API key under Chronicle (C); without one you get field notes. Crossed swords mark where each was fought. Replay the battle (or the whole campaign) plays the troop movements back on the map: pause, change speed, or click the bar to jump to any command; Esc returns to the game.

Allies & ground

End a command in a local chief's seat to win them over: they send one levy formation and add +1 defence in their sphere. Vegetation is from satellite land cover: thorn scrub and pine slow a march and give cover, and scrub forest (phulai and kao thicket) gives +2.
